How might UN analysts tell a quiet place from an unheard one?

Silence as a data point

Compare the reporting a place normally gets with what's arriving now. Check any silence against independent signals. Keep the gap attached to the analysis, all the way up to the envoy.

Scenario

A Northwest district usually gets about 15 reports a month, including local-language radio. This month: 2, both in English, both from capital outlets. Satellite shows newly burned farmland. Quiet, or unheard?

Why it could work

From DPPA and our research

  • DPPA's tools avoid "embedding analysis." This surfaces gaps and never concludes.
  • GeoGuard's conflict data had no category for farmer-herder clashes, so they never appeared on the map.
  • Doubt drops off as a claim moves from inputs to analysis to the envoy.
  • Conflict data comes mostly from news, which under-reports rural and local-language areas.

Already done elsewhere

  • Zero reporting: clinics report "no cases," so silence isn't mistaken for health.
  • Ships going dark: switched-off trackers hid about 6% of global fishing (source).
  • Shutdown monitors: IODA and #KeepItOn treat a region going silent as a warning.
  • El Fasher, 2025: under a blackout, satellite imagery showed what reporting couldn't.
